Daniel had been waiting months for his grandfather to visit Korea. He loved living there and wanted George to understand why.

Unfortunately, George was difficult to impress.

Their first stop was a huge department store in Seoul.

“Shopping in Korea is on another level,” Daniel said.

George looked at the crowds. “Too many people buying things they don’t need.”

At lunch, Daniel ordered Korean fried chicken.

“Okay. When it comes to fried chicken, Korea is hard to beat.”

George took one bite. “Too sweet.”

Daniel stared at him. “You liked it.”

“I said it was too sweet.”

That evening, Daniel showed him the subway system. George admitted the trains came frequently, but complained about the stairs. He liked the cafés, but thought the coffee was overpriced. He was impressed by late-night delivery, but said people should cook for themselves.

After two days, Daniel was exhausted.

Then, on their final morning, George woke up with a terrible rash on his arms and neck.

“It’s probably my eczema,” he said.

Daniel looked worried. “We should get it checked.”

“It’s not life-threatening.”

“I know. We’re still going.”

Daniel found a nearby dermatologist and made an appointment. They arrived expecting a long wait, but George was seen quickly. The dermatologist examined his skin, asked several questions, and explained that the dry weather and a new soap had probably irritated his eczema.

They were finished surprisingly fast.

Outside, George looked at the receipt.

“That’s it?”

“That’s it.”

“We made the appointment this morning.”

“Yes.”

“And I saw a specialist today?”

“Yes.”

George looked back at the clinic.

Daniel smiled cautiously. “In terms of convenient access to medical care, Korea has a pretty strong case.”

George didn’t answer.

Daniel waited for the complaint.

Finally, George said, “I’ll give you that.”

Daniel almost laughed. “Seriously?”

“Don’t get excited. Your coffee is still too expensive.”

“Fine. I can live with that.”

They started walking toward the subway.

After a few steps, George stopped.

“Daniel.”

“What?”

“Could we go back to that chicken place for lunch?”

Daniel stared at him.

“I thought it was too sweet.”

George shrugged. “I never said it was bad.”

For the first time all week, Daniel decided not to argue.
