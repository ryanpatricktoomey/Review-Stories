1. runner's high

달리기 같은 운동을 하면서 느끼는 행복감이나 편안함을 의미해요. 운동 중에 갑자기 피로가 덜 느껴지거나 기분이 좋아지는 경험을 말할 때 사용해요.

달리기뿐 아니라 다른 종류의 운동에서도 비슷한 느낌을 경험할 수 있어요.

2. cabin

산이나 숲속에 있는 작은 집이나 오두막을 뜻해요. 특히 등산이나 야외 활동 중 머무는 숙소를 가리킬 때 자주 사용해요.

3. my legs are falling off

다리가 너무 아프거나 지쳐서 떨어져 나갈 것 같다는 과장된 표현이에요.

• My legs feel like they're going to fall off!

• 다리가 떨어져 나갈 것 같아!

Based on a True Story: This story is based on American mountaineer Beck Weathers, who survived the deadly 1996 Mount Everest disaster. It is told in the first person, with thoughts and dialogue reconstructed for storytelling purposes. The major events are based on his documented experience.

I was standing near the summit of Mount Everest when I realized something was terribly wrong.

I couldn't see properly.

Years earlier, I'd had surgery on my eyes. Now, at nearly 8,000 meters above sea level, my vision had become dangerously blurry.

I was supposed to continue climbing with my team, but I couldn't safely move forward.

Our guide told me to wait while the others continued toward the summit.

So I waited.

Hours passed.

The weather began to change.

The wind grew stronger, and snow started falling. Soon, I could barely see anything.

I knew there were cabins at lower altitudes where climbers could rest, but they were far below me. Up here, there was nowhere to hide from the storm.

Then the blizzard hit.

The wind was so powerful that standing became difficult.

My hands were freezing. My face was numb.

I tried to move, but every step was exhausting.

At one point, I thought, "My legs are falling off!"

They were so cold and painful that I could barely feel them.

Eventually, other climbers found me and tried to help me descend.

But the storm was getting worse.

We became lost in the darkness, unable to find our camp.

I was completely exhausted.

There was no runner's high, no sudden burst of energy, nothing that could make the pain disappear.

I just wanted to lie down.

And eventually, I did.

I collapsed in the snow.

The others believed I was dying.

They left me behind.

For hours, I lay unconscious in the freezing darkness.

When I finally woke up, something strange happened.

I realized I was still alive.

My hands were badly frozen. My face was covered in ice.

But I could move.

I knew that if I stayed there, I would die.

So I stood up.

I could barely walk.

Every step was painful, and I had no idea whether I was going in the right direction.

But I kept moving.

Eventually, I saw the tents of our camp.

The climbers inside were shocked.

They had believed I was dead.

I had somehow walked back from a place where nobody expected me to survive.

I lost my right hand, most of my left hand, and part of my nose to frostbite.

But I survived.

And when I finally returned home, I understood something I hadn't fully appreciated before.

Reaching the summit had never been the most important thing.

Getting home alive was.
