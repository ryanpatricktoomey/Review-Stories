문자 그대로는 “기도가 응답받았다”라는 뜻이지만, 일상에서는 꼭 종교적인 의미가 아니라 간절히 원하던 것이 드디어 생겼을 때 과장해서 “내 소원이 이루어졌어!”처럼 쓸 수 있어요. 문맥에 따라 my prayers have been answered처럼 소유격을 붙이는 것이 자연스럽습니다.

The family had just started Chuseok lunch when Grandma placed three huge plates of jeon on the table.

There was zucchini jeon, fish jeon, and donggeurangttaeng, still warm from the pan.

Six-year-old Yuna stared at them.

“Grandma, I don’t want these.”

Grandma froze.

Her chopsticks remained in the air.

“You don’t... want jeon?”

“I—”

Grandma stood up.

“Yuna.”

Everyone became quiet.

And then Grandma began.

“Jeon on the table, still golden and hot,

You say you don’t want it? I think you forgot

The hours we spent by the stove in the heat,

Preparing these beautiful treasures to eat.

First comes the flour, a delicate coat,

Not too much flour—that point I must note.

Then into egg, bright yellow and fine,

Each little piece waits patiently in line.

Then comes the pan with its shimmering oil,

Where all of our careful preparation can spoil.

Too cold and it’s greasy, too hot and it burns;

A perfect jeon waits till the right moment turns.

Listen! That sizzle! That wonderful sound!

One side becomes golden and then turns around.

You watch for the edges, you check underneath,

While wonderful smells drift around you like wreaths.

The zucchini softens but still holds its shape.

The fish becomes tender beneath its egg cape.

The meat in the middle is juicy and round.

Three kinds of happiness! Look what we’ve found!

Nothing says Chuseok like standing all day

Beside a hot frying pan, cooking away.

Your clothes smell like oil, your hair smells like food,

And somehow that smell puts the whole house in the mood.

When I think of Chuseok, I think of this plate.

We start in the morning. We cook until late.

One person is frying, one brings the supplies,

And one steals the fresh ones when nobody spies.

Your father was famous for doing just that.

He’d enter the kitchen and quietly chat.

Then one jeon would vanish. Then two. Maybe three.

He thought we were blind, but your grandmother could see.

The first jeon is special, but let me explain:

A cold jeon tomorrow can rise once again.

Put it in a pan and warm it up slow,

And suddenly—ah!—there’s that beautiful glow.

Some people use microwaves. That may be fast,

But crisp little edges become things of the past.

Give jeon a pan and a minute or two.

Show it respect, and it will respect you.

And then there is soy sauce, waiting nearby,

With vinegar giving the flavor some bite.

A gentle small dip—not a bath, understand!

You don’t drown good jeon with a careless hand.

Jeon really captures our Chuseok for me:

Too much preparation, then eating with glee.

You make enough food for an army of ten,

And somehow by evening you’re frying again.

It wouldn’t be Chuseok without this great plate,

Still disappearing no matter how much we create.

And when someone brings fresh jeon straight from the pan,

My prayers have been answered! I’m its greatest fan!

So zucchini, fish, meat—whatever you choose,

With jeon at the table, nobody can lose.

Respect every piece, every sizzle, each smell—

And now, little Yuna, what have you to tell?”

Grandma stopped.

Nobody moved.

Yuna’s father stared at his rice.

Her uncle was still holding a piece of jeon halfway to his mouth.

Yuna looked up at Grandma.

“I like jeon.”

Grandma blinked.

“You just said you didn’t want it.”

“I said I don’t want jeon.”

“Yes!”

Yuna pointed toward the kitchen.

“I want the hot jeon. Grandpa said there’s more coming.”

Everyone turned.

Grandpa appeared from the kitchen holding a frying pan.

“Fresh batch.”

Yuna jumped out of her chair.

Grandma stared at her.

Then she looked at the steaming jeon.

“Move over.”

가족들이 막 추석 점심을 먹기 시작했을 때, 할머니가 커다란 전 접시 세 개를 식탁 위에 올려놓았다.

애호박전, 생선전, 동그랑땡이 있었고 아직 프라이팬의 온기가 남아 있었다.

여섯 살 유나는 전을 빤히 바라봤다.

“할머니, 나 전 안 먹을래요.”

할머니가 얼어붙었다.

“전을... 안 먹겠다고?”

“저는—”

이상하게도 그 냄새가 온 집을 명절 분위기로 만들어.

내가 추석을 생각하면 이 전 접시가 떠오른단다.

아침부터 시작해서 늦게까지 부치지.

한 사람은 전을 부치고, 한 사람은 재료를 가져오고,

또 한 사람은 아무도 안 볼 때 갓 부친 전을 훔쳐 먹고.

네 아빠가 그걸로 아주 유명했단다.

부엌에 들어와 조용히 이야기를 시작하지.

그러면 전 하나가 사라지고, 둘, 어쩌면 셋까지.

우리가 모르는 줄 알았겠지만 네 할머니 눈에는 다 보였어.

첫날 갓 부친 전은 특별하지만 이것도 알아 두렴.

다음 날 차가워진 전도 다시 살아날 수 있단다.

프라이팬에 넣고 천천히 데워 봐.

그러면 갑자기—아!—그 아름다운 빛깔이 돌아오지.

전자레인지를 쓰는 사람들도 있어. 빠르긴 하지.

하지만 바삭한 가장자리는 영영 사라지고 만단다.

전에게 프라이팬과 1~2분의 시간을 줘.

전을 존중하면 전도 너를 존중할 테니.

그리고 옆에는 간장이 기다리고 있지.

식초가 살짝 새콤한 맛을 더해 주고.

살짝 찍어 먹는 거야. 목욕시키는 게 아니란다!

훌륭한 전을 아무렇게나 간장에 빠뜨리는 게 아니야.

전은 내게 추석의 모습을 정말 잘 담아낸단다.

엄청나게 준비하고, 그다음 신나게 먹는 것.

열 명의 군대가 먹을 만큼 음식을 만들어 놓고도,

어째서인지 저녁이 되면 또 전을 부치고 있지.

이 훌륭한 전 접시가 없다면 추석이라고 할 수 없어.

아무리 많이 만들어도 계속 사라지는 전.

내 소원이 이루어진 거야! 나는 전의 열렬한 팬이니까!

애호박이든 생선이든 고기든 무엇을 고르든,

식탁에 전이 있다면 누구도 손해 볼 일이 없어.

자, 유나야. 이제 뭐라고 말하겠니?”

할머니가 멈췄다.

유나의 아빠는 자기 밥만 바라봤다.

삼촌은 전 한 조각을 입으로 가져가던 자세 그대로 멈춰 있었다.
