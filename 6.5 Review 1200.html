직역하면 “자기 등을 두드려 주다”이지만, 실제로는 스스로에게 잘했다고 칭찬하라는 뜻이에요.

I stood in the kitchen and stared at it.

It sat on the counter like it had been waiting for me all morning. Maybe all week. Maybe my entire adult life. I hated how small it looked. Big enemies should look big. They should have sharp teeth or at least dramatic lighting.

This was just a cup.

Still, my heart raced.

I told myself to calm down, which has never helped anyone calm down in the history of calming down. I folded my arms and looked away, but I could still feel it there. Quiet. Patient. Powerful in the most annoying way.

I had made a promise to myself last night. No more afternoon coffee. Not forever, maybe. I was not a hero. I was not trying to quit cold turkey and become the kind of person who says, “I only drink herbal tea now,” with a peaceful smile. I only wanted one afternoon without it.

One afternoon.

That sounded possible at midnight.

At 2:37 p.m., it sounded like war.

The problem was not even the caffeine. Not completely. It was the habit. The little ceremony. The warm cup in my hands. The bitter taste. The way I could pretend I was becoming productive instead of just drinking something with enough acidity to make my stomach complain.

A reasonable person would say, “Just don’t make it.”

I was not speaking to a reasonable person. I was speaking to myself.

“Rome wasn’t built in a day,” I whispered.

Then I immediately got angry. Rome again? Really? Rome had roads, armies, and history. I was fighting the urge to press one button on a coffee machine. There was no need to bring ancient civilization into this.

I stepped closer.

Then back.

Then closer again.

I hated this part of myself: the part that could understand a problem perfectly and still stand there like a confused statue. I knew what I needed to do. I knew it was small. That made it worse. If it had been impossible, I could have respected my failure.

Finally, I grabbed the cup.

For one terrible second, I held it near the machine.

Then I opened the cabinet, put the cup inside, and shut the door.

That was it.

No music played. No one clapped. I did not become a new person.

But I made tea instead, and after the first sip, I told myself to give yourself a pat on the back.

Not a big one.

Just enough.

오후 2시 37분에는 전쟁처럼 느껴졌다.

문제는 카페인만이 아니었다. 완전히는 아니었다. 문제는 습관이었다. 작은 의식 같은 것. 손에 쥔 따뜻한 컵. 쓴맛. 내 위가 불평할 만큼 산도가 있는 것을 마시면서, 내가 생산적인 사람이 되어 가고 있다고 믿는 척할 수 있다는 점.

이성적인 사람이라면 이렇게 말할 것이다. “그냥 만들지 마.”

하지만 나는 이성적인 사람과 대화하고 있는 게 아니었다. 나는 나 자신과 대화하고 있었다.

“로마는 하루아침에 세워지지 않았다고도 말했다.” 나는 속삭였다.

그리고 나는 바로 화가 났다. 또 로마라고? 정말? 로마에는 도로도 있었고, 군대도 있었고, 역사도 있었다. 나는 커피 머신 버튼 하나 누르고 싶은 충동과 싸우고 있었다. 여기에 고대 문명을 끌어들일 필요는 없었다.

나는 한 걸음 다가갔다.

그리고 물러났다.

그리고 다시 다가갔다.

나는 내 이런 부분이 싫었다. 문제를 완벽하게 이해하면서도 혼란스러운 동상처럼 서 있는 부분. 내가 무엇을 해야 하는지 알고 있었다. 그것이 작은 일이라는 것도 알고 있었다. 그래서 더 싫었다. 불가능한 일이었다면, 내 실패를 조금은 존중할 수 있었을 것이다.

그러고는 수납장을 열고, 컵을 안에 넣고, 문을 닫았다.

하지만 대신 차를 만들었고, 첫 모금을 마신 뒤 나는 나 자신에게 잘했다고 해줘야겠다고 생각했다.
