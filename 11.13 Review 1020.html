Imagine a man and a woman in their fifties appearing on television.

The man has gray hair, wrinkles, and a little extra weight. People describe him as handsome, mature, and distinguished.

The woman has gray hair, wrinkles, and a body that has changed with age. People wonder whether she's "let herself go."

Funny how growing older can make a man look more attractive but make a woman seem less desirable.

The Rules Nobody Talks About

Consider how differently we judge men's and women's appearances.

A bald man can look confident and masculine. A woman losing her hair may feel pressured to wear a wig.

A man who doesn't wear makeup looks normal. A woman without makeup might be asked if she's sick.

A man with gray hair looks distinguished. A woman with gray hair might be told she should dye it.

A man who gains weight is sometimes described as having a "dad bod." A woman who gains the same amount of weight may be criticized for not taking care of herself.

And the double standards don't disappear when people are young.

A young man who spends a lot of time on his appearance might be praised for taking care of himself. But a young woman who spends the same amount of time getting ready may be called vain or high-maintenance.

Women can be criticized for caring too much about their looks and for not caring enough.

You can't have it both ways.

Just Ignore What People Think?

When women complain about these expectations, they're often told to stop worrying about other people's opinions.

"Just be yourself! If someone doesn't like how you look, their loss!"

That's encouraging advice, but it's easier said than done.

Appearance can influence dating, job interviews, and even how seriously people take someone at work.

Imagine being a female flight attendant who is constantly reminded to look young, attractive, and perfectly groomed, while male colleagues face fewer expectations about their appearance.

After hearing comments about your weight, hairstyle, or makeup for years, you might finally think, "I've had it up to here. I can't hold it in anymore!"

You might even feel like I'm gonna explode when someone tells you that you're overreacting.

Are All Appearance Standards Unfair?

Not necessarily.

Different jobs sometimes require different clothing or grooming standards. And everyone, regardless of gender, experiences some pressure about appearance.

But we should be aware of whether those expectations are actually reasonable.

If an employer requires a professional appearance, shouldn't the basic standards apply equally to men and women?

And if someone wants to challenge unfair expectations, perhaps they should get in touch with their own assumptions first.

Do we criticize women for wearing too much makeup while also judging them for wearing none?

Do we praise men for aging naturally while expecting women to fight every sign of getting older?

Perhaps the biggest double standard isn't that we expect women to look beautiful.

It's that we expect them to look beautiful without appearing to make an effort.

And when they inevitably fail to satisfy everyone, we blame them for trying too hard—or not trying hard enough.
